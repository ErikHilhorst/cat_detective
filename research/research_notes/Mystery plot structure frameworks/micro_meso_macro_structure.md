# Micro / Meso / Macro Structure Frameworks for Mystery Plots and Clue Design

Scope: frameworks that split mystery and narrative design into nested scales (clue / room / case; beat / scene / sequence / act; one-fact inference / local theory / whole-case theory), with rules of thumb that can be applied to Cat Detective. Cat Detective mapping: **clue chip = micro**, **room + local deduction board = meso**, **case + final timeline board = macro**.

## 1. Established frameworks for nested story scale

### Takeaway
Screenwriting (McKee) and fiction craft (Swain, Maass) give a clean nested hierarchy: beat, scene, sequence, act, story. Each level is defined by a *value change* bigger than the level below it. Swain's scene/sequel adds an alternating goal-to-decision rhythm, and Maass's "micro-tension" covers the line-level layer. None of these is mystery-specific, but they map directly onto clue, room and case.

### Cited Findings
- **McKee's hierarchy, smallest to largest:** beat, scene, sequence, act, story. A value change sits at the core of each level. — [Shortform: McKee Story Structure 101](https://www.shortform.com/blog/robert-mckee-story-structure/)
- **Beat:** "a single exchange of action and reaction between characters that shifts the emotional charge in a scene either positively or negatively." — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/robert-mckee-story-structure/)
- **Scene:** "action through conflict in more or less continuous time and space that turns the value charged condition of a character's life on at least one value with a degree of perceptible significance." A scene in which nothing changes is "a non event." — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/robert-mckee-story-structure/); [Ben Tylecote's notes on McKee](https://bentylecote.blogspot.com/2024/08/story-robert-mckee-my-notes.html)
- **Sequence:** a series of scenes, "generally two to five," that "culminates with greater impact than any previous scene." — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/robert-mckee-story-structure/)
- **Act:** a series of sequences that "peaks in a climactic scene which causes a major reversal of values, more powerful in its impact than any previous sequence or scene." — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/robert-mckee-story-structure/)
- **Story:** the whole arc reaches "a major, irreversible shift in value." — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/robert-mckee-story-structure/)
- **Caveat:** Shortform argues McKee's idea that every beat serves several larger value changes may be "more complex and confusing than it needs to be." Writers also disagree on exactly what a "scene" is. — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/robert-mckee-story-structure/)
- **Swain's scene and sequel:**
  - A scene is "a unit of conflict, an account of an effort to attain a goal despite opposition." It runs goal, conflict, disaster.
  - A sequel runs reaction, dilemma, decision. It "translate[s] disaster into a goal, telescope[s] reality, and control[s] tempo."
  - The decision sets up the next scene's goal. More scene means faster pacing; more sequel means slower pacing.
  - — [Wikipedia: Scene and sequel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scene_and_sequel); [Advanced Fiction Writing (Ingermanson)](https://www.advancedfictionwriting.com/blog/2009/05/25/scenes-sequels-and-chapters/); [TCK Publishing](https://www.tckpublishing.com/scene-and-sequel/). These are secondary sources; Swain's own book text was not retrieved.
- **Maass's micro-tension:**
  - Tension at the line level, keeping the reader uncertain about "the next few seconds," not the wider story.
  - It comes from contradictions, dilemmas and clashing impulses inside a character in the moment.
  - His test: shuffle the manuscript pages and check that each page has micro-tension.
  - — [Writer Unboxed (Maass)](https://writerunboxed.com/?p=3681); [RMFW](https://rmfw.org/?p=44372); [Live Write Thrive](https://www.livewritethrive.com/?p=33485)
  - A critic calls the "constant tension" claim hyperbole. — [Novel Journey](https://noveljourney.blogspot.com/2012/12/donald-maass-on-micro-tension-and.html)
- **The detective "double plot":** the story of the crime in the past is uncovered through the story of the investigation in the present. Clues work by metonymy, linking cause to effect and past to present. — [Wikipedia: Clues in detective fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clues_in_detective_fiction)

### Inferences
- **A three-layer mapping for Cat Detective:**
  - **Micro (beat):** one dialogue page or bracket unlock. It should shift *something*: a new fact, a suspicion, or a character tell. Maass's micro-tension applies to dialogue pages, so each page should carry a small contradiction or withheld detail.
  - **Meso (scene/sequence):** a room. It is a two-to-five-interaction sequence that peaks in the local solve, and the solve is the room's value turn: suspicion moves onto or off someone.
  - **Macro (act/story):** the case. The irreversible value change is the final board, where the false "bird alive at 8" becomes a staged squawk.
- The forced bedroom-before-living-room order is an act-level reversal in McKee's terms. The bedroom builds a value (the bird was fine at 8) and the cage room inverts it.
- Swain's sequel maps onto the *post-solve* moment: reaction (the toast "X might have some explaining to do"), dilemma (who to revisit), decision (walk to the confrontation). The `requiresSolve` toast acts as the sequel's "decision" prompt, which makes it a good structural anchor.
- The double plot maps exactly onto Cat Detective: the true timeline (crime story) versus room traversal order (investigation story). The final board makes the player rebuild the crime story from investigation-ordered fragments.

## 2. Tabletop RPG clue-flow structure (Alexandrian, GUMSHOE)

### Takeaway
The two dominant tabletop answers to "players get stuck" are:
- **The Alexandrian:** redundancy (the Three Clue Rule), non-linear node graphs (the inverted Three Clue Rule), revelation lists for diagnosis, and proactive fallbacks.
- **GUMSHOE:** guaranteed delivery of *core* clues/leads, with optional clues layered for depth.

Both separate "information needed to progress" from "information that enriches."

### Cited Findings
- **The Three Clue Rule:** "For any conclusion you want the PCs to make, include at least three clues."
  - Rationale: each clue has several failure points (search the right spot, care to examine it, pass a check, draw the right inference). Players "will probably miss the first clue, ignore the second, and misinterpret the third."
  - — [The Alexandrian: Three Clue Rule](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/1118/roleplaying-games/three-clue-rule)
- **Extension:** any chokepoint should have at least three solutions; non-chokepoint problems need only one. — [Three Clue Rule](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/1118/roleplaying-games/three-clue-rule)
- **Corollaries in the same article:**
  - *Permissive clue-finding:* reward clever approaches you hadn't planned for, and treat prepared clues as a safety net, not a straitjacket.
  - *Proactive clues:* "Have a guy with a gun walk through the door" (Chandler). When players stall, something should happen to them that yields new clues.
  - *Red herrings are overrated:* players invent their own suspects anyway.
  - *Nothing is foolproof.*
  - — [Three Clue Rule](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/1118/roleplaying-games/three-clue-rule)
- **Revelations** (credited to Ben Robbins): the facts players must learn. A revelation list "should be a checklist or a trigger, not the whole explanation."
  - Method: list each conclusion and its clues, then tick off clues as found.
  - Tick off a conclusion only when players actually *reach* it, not when they first consider it.
  - Add clues when a conclusion stalls.
  - — [Three Clue Rule](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/1118/roleplaying-games/three-clue-rule)
- **Clue list vs revelation list:**
  - The *clue list* (design time) shows the clues a node contains and where they point. It is used to check that every node holds three or more.
  - The *revelation list* (play time) shows the clues that *point to* a node. What matters is "whether they've missed all the clues" to a node, not any single one.
  - Design backward: "Look at a node and ask, 'How would the PCs get there?'"
  - — [Alexandrian: Using Revelation Lists (2018)](https://thealexandrian.net/?p=40978)
- **Structural vs background revelations:** keep background revelations on a separate list from structural ones, and track only the structural list in play. — [Alexandrian search results, revelation-list posts](https://thealexandrian.net/?p=46338). This comes from a search summary; the full page was not fetched.
- **Inverted Three Clue Rule:** "If the PCs have access to ANY three clues, they will reach at least ONE conclusion." — [Alexandrian: Node-Based Scenario Design pt. 2](https://thealexandrian.net/creations/misc/node-design/node-design2.html)
  - Worked example: a start node with clues to A, B and C; four nodes and nine clues in total.
  - Clues pointing to visited nodes are discarded, and the remaining clues to *unvisited* nodes decide whether progress continues.
  - The same nine clues built as a linear plot are less flexible; node design gets "more mileage out of the same amount of work."
- **Node structures (part 5):** — [Node-Based Scenario Design pt. 5](https://www.thealexandrian.net/creations/misc/node-design/node-design5.html)
  - *Conclusion:* feeder nodes each point to one concluding node.
  - *Funnel:* free-form layers converge on a chokepoint that opens the next layer.
  - *Layer cake:* each node points within its layer plus one clue to the next layer. Players drift between layers more naturally than in a funnel.
  - *Loop:* every node points to the others, so players can enter anywhere.
  - *Dead end:* clues pointing to it count only as "bonus clues."
  - Rule: "each node [has] a minimum of three clues in it and a minimum of three clues pointing to it."
- **Existential vs access leads:**
  - Existential leads show a node exists or matters. Access leads show how to get there.
  - If your three clues are mostly existential, "you haven't actually fulfilled the Three Clue Rule."
  - Foreshadow existential leads to build anticipation, then pay off with access. Leads can be nested in chains.
  - — [Alexandrian: Running Mysteries - The Two Types of Leads](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/50095/roleplaying-games/running-mysteries-the-two-types-of-leads)
- **GUMSHOE core clues** (Robin Laws, 2007): "If a scene contains a core clue and a player character uses an investigative ability that relates to the clue, the character will find the clue."
  - A core clue is needed to progress. A merely useful clue (e.g. how to kill the monster) is not core.
  - — [Wikipedia: Gumshoe System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gumshoe_System); [Pelgrane: Mo' Information, Mo' Troubles](https://pelgranepress.com/2011/11/24/mo-information-mo-troubles)
- **Pelgrane's four-tier information taxonomy:** — [Pelgrane: A Taxonomy of Investigations (2023)](https://pelgranepress.com/2023/03/08/a-taxonomy-of-investigations/)
  - **Leads** point to scenes; "GUMSHOE scenarios are structured around leads." Core leads are guaranteed; 0-point leads are optional routes or dead ends.
  - **Clues** give context, means and motive. Core clues are fundamental.
  - **Hints** are fragments that make sense only in retrospect. "There's no such thing as a core hint." A hint "opens more questions than it answers."
  - **Details** are background only. "There are no core details."

### Inferences
- **Cat Detective already follows the GUMSHOE philosophy at the micro level.** Bracket keywords auto-unlock on reading, which guarantees delivery the way core clues do. The remaining failure points are "did I talk to this object" and "did I draw the inference." So the Three Clue Rule should be applied to *inferences* (board answers), not to discovery.
- **Treat each board slot as an Alexandrian "conclusion."** Keep a revelation list per slot: which sources make the correct chip interpretable? For example, WHEN-1 (6:30) depends on the registry, the schedule's 7:00 call and Reyes's hunch. Each answer should be *supported* by two or three independent sources, even though only one source unlocks the chip.
- **Rooms are nodes and doorways are links.** The Malibu map is mostly a loop after the living room, with a funnel at the start (`RequiresVisited: bedroom` forces the bedroom node first). Cross-room character references ("Coco's squeaky wheels") are *existential leads*: they tell the player a fact lives elsewhere. Transfers are *access*. A cross-room confrontation gate should have the existential lead planted before the gate opens.
- **Pelgrane's tiers mapped to Cat Detective:**
  - **Core clues** = local and final board answers, which must have ungated sources (already enforced by `verify_level.py`).
  - **Hints** = confrontation slips like `hall_rumble_no_soup` that "open more questions." These should never be answers, which matches the existing rule.
  - **Details** = flavor text.
- **Proactive clues = the solve-gated toast.** If a player stalls, a scheduled event (a character walks up to the cat, or a new speech bubble appears) is the video-game equivalent of "a guy with a gun." Consider a soft fallback, e.g. a hint after N wrong submits.
- **Separate structural and background revelation lists:** structural = board answers; background = story-bible lore. Only the structural list needs the three-source redundancy check.

- I did not retrieve the original Pelgrane "core clues vs optional clues" article; the URL I tried returned 404. Its specifics come from Wikipedia and the 2023 taxonomy article instead.
- Alexandrian's "inverting conclusions" as a distinct named technique was not found in the Three Clue Rule article. The closest concept is the *inverted* Three Clue Rule above.

## 3. Clue taxonomy and how small deductions roll up

### Takeaway
No single authoritative taxonomy exists. A workable synthesis is:
- **Physical, testimonial and thematic clues** (by medium)
- **Rational vs intuitive clues** (inconsistency vs psychology)
- **Transposed or absent objects** (negative evidence)
- **Red herrings**, which are often true clues pointing to a wrong solution

Clues should be individually *non-conclusive* and gain force only in combination, which is the mechanism for rolling micro facts up into macro theory.

### Cited Findings
- **By medium:** one guide splits clues into "physical, verbal, and thematic" types (physical: fingerprints to DNA). Others list objects such as letters, tickets, diaries and texts. — [The Write Practice: Mystery Clues](https://thewritepractice.com/mystery-clues/); [Writing-World: Clues](https://writing-world.com/mystery/clues.shtml)
- Clues can be "physical evidence, testimony, sounds, smells, observations or actions." — [Wikipedia-derived entry via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clues_in_detective_fiction)
- **Testimonial discrepancies** are a named clue-planting technique, the closest match to "contradiction clue." — [ProWritingAid: Murder mystery clues](https://prowritingaid.com/murder-mystery-clues). Search snippet only.
- **Transposed object:** an item where it doesn't belong, or missing from where it should be, which is in practice the absence or negative clue. — [The Write Practice](https://thewritepractice.com/mystery-clues/). Search snippet only.
- **Red herring:** "any piece of information that distracts readers from true clues." One source notes "a single clue can be both a clue to the right solution and a red herring to the wrong solution." — [The Write Practice](https://thewritepractice.com/mystery-clues/)
- **Rational vs intuitive clues:**
  - *Rational clues* "do not fit into the wider picture," revealing inconsistencies in material evidence or statements. This is the contradiction clue in academic terms.
  - *Intuitive clues* concern imagining the criminal's state of mind and motive.
  - The same article also distinguishes red herrings from meaningless coincidences, and "simple markers" (unflagged details) from decodable clues.
  - — [Wikipedia: Clues in detective fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clues_in_detective_fiction)
- **Non-conclusive clues** (Richard York): clues generally shouldn't amount to proof beyond reasonable doubt, which would dispel the mystery too easily. — [Wikipedia: Clues in detective fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clues_in_detective_fiction)
- **Fair play:** Van Dine (1928): "All clues must be plainly stated and described." Knox (1929): "the detective is bound to declare any clues which he may discover." No vital clue may be revealed at the last moment. — [Wikipedia: Clues in detective fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clues_in_detective_fiction)
- **Pelgrane's hints** are an explicitly *retrospective* clue type: fragments that make sense only after related clues are found. — [Pelgrane: A Taxonomy of Investigations](https://pelgranepress.com/2023/03/08/a-taxonomy-of-investigations/)
- **Chained clues:** Golden Idol's internal "thought path" scheme links clues in a chain (key, then door, then room, then name). The developers admit they "failed to create a reliable framework for mystery building." — [Game Developer: Pursuing the "Aha!" moment](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/case-of-the-golden-idol)
- **Elimination as roll-up:** in Golden Idol's "Murder at the Little Mermaid," the cause of death is obvious and several suspects have weapons or motives. A bloody wall message is a red herring, and clues gradually *eliminate* suspects via access and alibi. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/case-of-the-golden-idol)

### Inferences
- **A proposed working taxonomy for Cat Detective clue authoring** (synthesis, not from one source), with Malibu examples:

  | Type | Definition | Malibu example |
  |---|---|---|
  | Physical | object state | broken latch, dropped glove |
  | Testimonial | what a character asserts | Derek heard the squawk at 8 |
  | Document | stated record | manifest entry, schedule |
  | Timeline | a bare timestamp, its own chip | `time_700pm` |
  | Contradiction / rational | two facts that cannot both be true | call at 7:00 vs inside by 6:30 |
  | Absence / transposed | the missing 6:00 snack; cases that are EMPTY | |
  | Psychological / intuitive | motive and tells | 47 ruined takes, "stopped complaining" |
  | Hint / slip | retrospective, opens questions | confrontation clues |
  | Red herring | true fact, wrong theory | Basil's latch |

- **Roll-up rule:**
  - **Micro:** one chip = one fact. This matches the existing "one clue = one fact" rule, and York's non-conclusive principle backs it.
  - **Meso:** a local board combines three to five micro facts into a room theory. That theory should still be non-conclusive (end on the suspicion).
  - **Macro:** the final board combines meso theories plus timeline chips into an elimination and contradiction proof.

  In this structure contradictions are the highest-value micro clues, because they are what turn facts into deductions.
- **Use the dual-purpose red herring.** Cat Detective already makes red herrings true facts (Basil really did break the latch). That keeps fair play intact, since nothing is a lie, while still misdirecting.
- **Mark which micro clues are hints vs core** (in the editor or verifier) so authors can see whether a room's board is supported by enough *core* facts.

- I found no authoritative source using "negative clue" or "contradiction clue" as formal categories. Those labels are practitioner synthesis.

## 4. Micro vs macro deductions in deduction video games (Golden Idol, Obra Dinn)

### Takeaway
Modern deduction games split a case into small, verifiable sub-deductions with feedback, nested inside a larger whole-case confirmation.
- **Golden Idol** uses fill-in-the-blank scrolls with near-miss feedback ("two or fewer incorrect") plus naming and custom sub-puzzles.
- **Obra Dinn** batches confirmation in groups of three fates to block guessing, and uses chapters to make the timeline legible.

Both treat brute-force prevention and "aha" pacing as core design constraints.

### Cited Findings
- **Golden Idol's input:**
  - Players fill phrases into text that provides "grammatical and semantic context," with a limited phrase set per scenario.
  - Early tests of building full sentences from generic phrases failed. Players preferred "here is a bunch of phrases, where do you think they fit?"
  - — [Game Developer: Pursuing the "Aha!" moment](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/case-of-the-golden-idol)
- **Golden Idol's feedback:**
  - Whole-scroll right/wrong judgment frustrated players in complex scenarios, so the team added "two or fewer slots are incorrect."
  - That indicator rewarded getting close but made the game easier and shorter.
  - Per-slot validation was avoided because it invites brute-forcing.
  - — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/case-of-the-golden-idol)
- **Golden Idol's meso layer:** players identify the people in a scene and solve a custom puzzle per scenario, which "gave them a sense of progress." These are sub-deductions before the main scroll. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/case-of-the-golden-idol)
- **Golden Idol's evidence budget:**
  - Evidence is minimal: only items necessary to the solution or deliberate misdirection.
  - Some scenarios allow multiple routes to the same conclusion.
  - Clues were added until flow was good and removed when a scenario became trivial (a will was obscured because it made a case too easy).
  - Playtesting was done "at least five to seven times."
  - — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/case-of-the-golden-idol)
- **Golden Idol's complexity limit:** the developers reportedly hit a "roof of complexity" past which puzzles stopped being fun or functional. — [Thinky Games feature](https://thinkygames.com/features/how-the-case-of-the-golden-idol-developers-made-one-of-the-decades-best-detective-games-twice/). Search snippet only; the page returned 403 on fetch.
- **Obra Dinn's confirmation:**
  - Correct fates are checked in groups of three, "to deter guesswork." The last six fates are checked in pairs.
  - Each of the 60 people has a name, a cause of death and a killer, and some deaths accept more than one answer.
  - — [Wikipedia: Return of the Obra Dinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_the_Obra_Dinn)
- **Obra Dinn's chapters:**
  - Ten narrative events divide the deaths into chapters.
  - Pope added them after playtesters were confused by out-of-order deaths, and the chapter structure led to the logbook as timeline plus crew catalogue.
  - — [Wikipedia: Return of the Obra Dinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_the_Obra_Dinn)
- **Reviewers' reading of the batching:** it forces players to be "at least 90% sure," and partial results still reveal which entries are wrong. — [God is a Geek review](https://www.godisageek.com/reviews/return-of-the-obra-dinn-review/); [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/thoughts-return-of-the-obra-dinn). This is reviewer interpretation, not a Pope quote.

### Inferences
- **Cat Detective's three scales line up with these games:**
  - **Micro:** the chip (Obra Dinn: a single field such as name or cause).
  - **Meso:** the local room board (Golden Idol: the naming and sub-puzzles; Obra Dinn: a chapter).
  - **Macro:** the final timeline board (Golden Idol: the full scroll; Obra Dinn: the full manifest).
- **Feedback granularity:** the current "Incorrect logic - 2/3 fit" sits between Golden Idol's thresholded "two or fewer wrong" and Obra Dinn's batch confirmation.
  - A per-count readout on a *seven*-slot final board is close to per-slot validation once players iterate. Golden Idol explicitly avoided that because of brute-forcing.
  - Option: on the final board, show the count only when 2 or fewer slots are wrong, or require all slots of one category to be right before reporting.
  - This is a design option, not a finding.
- **Obra Dinn's chapter lesson matches the Evening timeline.** Out-of-order information confused players until a chronological scaffold existed. Cat Detective's chronological WHERE/WHEN sort and "The Evening" notes page play the same role.
- **Golden Idol's minimal-evidence rule supports Cat Detective's per-room decoy budget.** Every chip should be either an answer or deliberate misdirection; pure noise should be cut. Prune clues that trivialize a board, the way the will was obscured.

### Gaps
- No primary Lucas Pope quote on why he chose groups of three was retrieved.
- Golden Idol's "thought path" tool mechanics, per-scenario clue counts and slot counts were not found in the fetched sources.
- No source found uses the explicit labels "micro-deduction" or "macro-deduction." They are design shorthand.

## 5. Practical heuristics: clue counts, redundancy, bottlenecks

### Takeaway
Rules of thumb that converge across sources:
- Three sources per required conclusion.
- Three clues in and three clues out per node.
- Count only clues pointing at unvisited nodes.
- Bonus or dead-end clues don't count toward the minimum.
- Guarantee core information while gating enrichment.
- Prefer partial feedback that rewards closeness without enabling brute force.
- Have proactive fallbacks for stalls.
- Iterate the clue count by playtesting rather than formula.

### Cited Findings
- **Three per conclusion; three solutions per chokepoint; one for non-chokepoints.** — [Three Clue Rule](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/1118/roleplaying-games/three-clue-rule)
- **Node density:** three or more clues in each node and three or more pointing to it. Clues to clue-light nodes (fewer than three) are bonus clues. Prep only through the next layer. — [Node-Based Scenario Design pt. 5](https://www.thealexandrian.net/creations/misc/node-design/node-design5.html)
- **Count unvisited only:** progress is judged by the clues remaining to *unvisited* nodes. — [Node-Based Scenario Design pt. 2](https://thealexandrian.net/creations/misc/node-design/node-design2.html)
- **Existential vs access:** three existential leads don't satisfy the rule; access must be provided. — [Two Types of Leads](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/50095/roleplaying-games/running-mysteries-the-two-types-of-leads)
- **Track the whole set, not each clue:** worry only when *every* clue to a node has been missed. — [Using Revelation Lists](https://thealexandrian.net/?p=40978)
- **Red herrings sparingly:** players generate their own. — [Three Clue Rule](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/1118/roleplaying-games/three-clue-rule)
- **Guarantee core, optionalize depth:** "There's no such thing as a core hint." — [Pelgrane Taxonomy](https://pelgranepress.com/2023/03/08/a-taxonomy-of-investigations/)
- **Tune by playtest:** add clues until flow is good, remove them when trivial, and offer multiple routes to one conclusion. The near-miss threshold makes the game easier and shorter, which is a trade-off. — [Game Developer: Golden Idol](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/case-of-the-golden-idol)
- **Batch confirmation deters guessing.** — [Wikipedia: Obra Dinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_the_Obra_Dinn)
- **Every scene must turn a value.** A scene with no change is a "non event." — [Shortform on McKee](https://www.shortform.com/blog/robert-mckee-story-structure/)

### Inferences
Actionable checklist for Cat Detective (synthesis):
1. **Per board slot (conclusion):** at least one ungated chip source, which is already enforced, plus two or three *supporting* sources that make the correct interpretation reachable, ideally in different rooms or media (one physical, one testimony, one document). Write a revelation list per slot.
2. **Per room (node):**
   - At least three clues pointing *out* (existential leads to other rooms or characters).
   - At least three ways to learn *of* the room's key fact.
   - Every interaction must turn something (the McKee non-event test), which matches the existing "every flavor object yields some clue" rule.
3. **Per case (macro):**
   - The final theory should be reachable via more than one room order. Funnel only where the reversal needs it (bedroom before living room).
   - Check that no single room is the *sole* holder of a final-board-critical inference. If one is, add redundancy (Three Clue Rule extension: chokepoints need three solutions).
4. **Contradictions are the deduction engine.**
   - Design at least one explicit contradiction per local board: testimony vs physical, or document vs document.
   - Design one *cross-room* contradiction per final-board WHEN slot (6:30 vs the 7:00 call; the squawk at 8 vs the cage evidence).
5. **Decoys:** every chip should be either an answer or deliberate misdirection (Golden Idol). Red herrings are true facts supporting a wrong theory, never lies (fair play).
6. **Feedback:** keep the partial-count feedback on local boards, which are small and teach. Consider a thresholded or batched readout on the seven-slot final board to avoid brute force.
7. **Stall fallback:** a proactive event (the solve-gated toast, or a character approaching) plays the role of "a guy with a gun." Consider a soft hint after repeated wrong submits.
8. **Track structural vs background separately:** board answers get redundancy checks; story-bible lore doesn't.
