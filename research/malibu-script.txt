CASE FILE 01: The Missing Macaw
1. Case Overview
Setting: A luxurious, sun-drenched Malibu mansion with a "Studio Ghibli" painted aesthetic. Lush plants, warm lighting, rich woodwork, and sparkling blue water.
Tone: Cozy, humorous, and low-stakes. A clash between chaotic, egotistical Hollywood personalities and a chill, judgmental housecat. No murder, no gore—just a stolen, highly obnoxious parrot.
The Victim: "Rudebeak" the Macaw. A prized, expensive, and exceptionally foul-mouthed bird belonging to the Movie Star.
2. The Truth (What Actually Happened)
The Culprit: The Sound Guy.
The Motive: Rudebeak kept squawking and ruining the audio takes for the documentary crew. The Sound Guy, desperate for a perfect portfolio to break into Hollywood, snapped.
The Method (The Trick): At 7:00 PM, when the crew first arrived, the Sound Guy stashed Rudebeak inside one of his heavy, sound-proofed Acoustic Cases and moved it to the Bedroom. To cover his tracks, he ran an Audio Cable from his mixing board in the Library to a hidden speaker tucked under the birdcage in the Living Room. At 8:00 PM, he triggered a loop of Rudebeak squawking from the mixer. Everyone heard it — creating a fake time of crime that gave everyone, including himself, a false alibi.
Pre-crime subplot: Earlier that afternoon, the Gardener had already broken the birdcage latch himself in an attempt to free the bird into the wild. The bird didn't fly. The Gardener gave up. The Sound Guy found the cage already unlatched and simply lifted Rudebeak out — no tools needed.
3. Character Roster (The 10 Suspects)
The cat cannot converse with them; clicking them yields contextual observations or overheard mumbling.
The Movie Star (Living Room): Distraught, genuine, weeping on the couch.
The Husband (Bedroom): Nervous wreck. Hiding in the bedroom wearing heavy headphones to escape the noise of the house. Tried to speak to the Sound Guy in the hallway at ~7:00 PM and was brushed off.
The Manager (Entrance): A slick, horrible Hollywood agent. Screaming on his phone. Hates animals.
The Socialite (Pool Area): Perpetually bored, wearing massive sunglasses. Saw the Sound Guy hauling heavy cases through the garden at 6:45 PM. Didn't think much of it until now.
The Maid (Kitchen): Fiercely protective of the kitchen. Furious that the bird insulted her cooking.
The Gardener (Garden): A gentle nature lover trimming hedges. Quietly guilty — he snapped the cage latch earlier that day, trying to free the bird. The bird didn't leave. He never told anyone.
The Director (Library): Sleazy TLC-style documentary director. Thrilled by the drama because it means good TV ratings.
The Sound Guy (Library): Sweaty, wearing big headphones, hunched over a mixing board. Desperate for a clean audio take.
The Bird Handler (Living Room): Eccentric, obsessed with the bird's plumage. Frantically picking up loose feathers.
The Police Officer (Entrance): Grumpy, standing guard. Annoyed he was called in for a "missing poultry" case.

6. The Final Solve (Macro Deduction)
The Setup:
Once all 7 room puzzles are completed, a "Solve Case" button unlocks. The player opens the Notebook to the Final Case file. The right-hand tabs are populated only with the Macro Clues earned from the rooms.
If the player selects a Macro Clue, the Inspector Panel displays the text from that room's solved sentence as a reminder.
The Deduction Chain the Player Must Build:
Step 1 — Establish the fake alibi:
[8:00 PM Squawk] + [Hidden Speaker] + [Audio Cable → Living Room] = the squawk at 8PM was played through a speaker from the mixer. It was a recording.
Step 2 — Prove the bird was already gone:
[Sound-Proof Cases] = the bird could not have made a sound if it was inside. Combined with [Settled Feathers], it was gone well before 8PM.
Step 3 — Place the culprit at the scene:
[D. Marsh — 7:00 PM] + [D. Marsh = Sound Guy] + [Husband saw someone at 7:00 PM] + [Eyewitness at 6:47 PM] = the Sound Guy was moving cases through the garden before 7PM and signed them into the bedroom at 7PM.
Step 4 — Expose the red herrings:
[The Gardener] + [Broken Latch] = he snapped the latch, but the bird didn't leave. The Sound Guy used the already-open cage.
[The Maid] + [Insulted Cooking] = motive only, no physical evidence.
The Final Mad Libs Sentence (Left Page) — 5 slots:
[ WHO ] took Rudebeak at [ WHEN ], hiding the bird inside [ WHAT ], then played a [ HOW ] through a [ WHERE ] to fake an alibi.
The Solution:
[ The Sound Guy ] took Rudebeak at [ 7:00 PM ], hiding the bird inside [ Sound-Proof Cases ], then played an [ Audio Recording ] through a [ Hidden Speaker ] to fake an alibi.
Red herrings in the clue pool: [The Maid], [Insulted Cooking], [The Gardener], [Broken Latch], [PR Disaster], [Lockdown at 8:15 PM] — present to ensure the player must actually reason through the evidence rather than guess. The Gardener trap is the deepest: he has both physical motive and physical evidence, but the timeline collapses — the bird didn't leave when he broke the latch. Only the Sound Guy had the timing, the equipment, and the means to fake the sound.
