# Framing the case - opening and finale (design proposal)

Problem (playtest): the case starts at the entrance with exposition and no stakes
("the macaw is missing - so what?"), and ends on a five-beat epilogue that confirms the
solve but has no drama. The research report says the same thing from two sides:
the Act 1 break must *define the problem* (Espenson), the TV teaser shows the crime, and
the ending needs a gathering, a walk-back and a button (Christie / Johnson / Takumi).

Both ends should frame the case as a **case contract**: the opening tells the player the
case's questions and who might answer them; the finale answers them in public, one suspect
at a time. The opening's questions and the finale's beats should be the same list, so the
finale reads as a payoff of the opening.

---

## Opening: cold open -> stakes -> roll call -> the gate

Target length: 60-90 seconds, all skippable (Esc), each screen one short beat
(playtest 5: keep intros short and light on noir - this proposal stays within that
by being many tiny screens, not one long page).

### 1. Cold open (the teaser - show the crime, plant the lie)
Two or three cards, before the poster. Pure atmosphere, no Dikkie yet.

- **8:00 PM** - the living room, warm lamplight. A loud **"BRAWK!"** cuts through the
  house. Vivienne, mid-affirmation, flinches. Everyone hears it.
- **8:15 PM** - Petra goes to the cage with the evening treats. The cage is empty.
  The latch hangs crooked. A scream. Reyes: "Nobody leaves."

This **plants the Big Lie** (alive at 8) before the player has touched anything. The
bedroom then *reinforces* it and the cage room *breaks* it - the strongest arc in the case
gets its first beat on screen one. (Note: keep it neutral - show the squawk as heard, never
show where it came from.)

### 2. Stakes (why should I care?)
Stack three stakes, one line each, cozy-scaled:

1. **A life:** Rudebeak is out there somewhere tonight, alive and frightened.
2. **A clock:** the lockdown only holds until **sunrise** - at dawn Reyes has to open
   the gates and the crew trucks roll out for the premiere. Whatever (whoever) is hidden
   in this house leaves with them. *Narrative clock only - no timer mechanic.* It also
   explains the culprit's plan (wheel the cases out on the trucks) without naming him.
3. **An innocent:** Reyes already has an easy answer - and an easy answer means
   somebody innocent takes the fall. (Option A: say nothing more. Option B: name the
   obvious suspect - e.g. "the maid had the keys" - the Christie "obvious suspect"
   device; it gives the player a reason to care about clearing herrings, at the
   cost of tilting Rosa early.)

Then the existing poster + "The cat took the case." (The current intro page can be
trimmed into this sequence.)

### 3. Roll call (meet everyone before entering)
The cast introduced as **cards**, grouped, 2-4 per screen, using the existing character
sprites as portraits. Each card: name, role, **one hook** (a planted question, never a
conclusion).

In-world device options (pick one):
- **a. The news van at the gate** - a late-night entertainment reporter doing a live
  stand-up: "Inside the Vale house tonight..." Fits Chip's PR panic and the premiere
  stakes; tone is breezy.
- **b. Reyes's notepad** - polaroids clipped to a pad, Reyes muttering a line each.
  Grumpier, sets up Reyes as the player's interpreter.
- **c. The documentary crew's call sheet** - who was booked tonight. In-world, but dry,
  and it competes with the pool area's crumpled schedule (a real clue) - avoid.

Recommendation: **a** for the household, with Reyes's grumble as the last line.

Draft hooks (fair-play rules: the culprit is introduced here, but hidden in the list -
Christie's "hide the key item among more memorable ones"; herrings get the juiciest
hooks):

| Group | Card | Hook (planted question) |
|-------|------|-------------------------|
| The family | Vivienne Vale, movie star | "Rudebeak was her co-star. Lately, he'd been stealing her scenes." |
| | Derek, her husband | "Has not been seen downstairs all evening." |
| The household | Rosa, the maid | "Holds every key in the house." |
| | Basil, the gardener | "Has opinions about cages." |
| | Petra, the bird handler | "The last person to feed Rudebeak." |
| The production | Wexler, the director | "Shooting a documentary about the star. Tonight's footage just got better." |
| | The crew (sound, lights, grips) | "Half a dozen people, carts and cases everywhere." *(the Sound Guy appears here, unnamed, as part of the crew - present, not pointed at)* |
| | Chip Sterling, manager | "Thinks about headlines. Always." |
| Guests | Coco, socialite | "Never puts her phone down." |
| The law | Officer Reyes | "Did not want this case." |

Notes:
- The Sound Guy needs to be *present* in the roll call (Knox 1 / Van Dine 10: the culprit
  must be someone met early) but not *featured*. A crew group card does both; the
  pacing rule (no Marsh evidence before the library) still holds because no *evidence*
  is given.
- Every hook should pay off in a room (each is a micro setup with a known meso payoff -
  see the room contracts). Hooks that never pay off are worse than no hook.

### 4. The gate (the case questions, stated out loud)
Reyes at the front door, one page, as the Act 1 break:
"Bird's gone. Doors are sealed. Somebody in there is lying. Find out **who took him,
how he got out of a locked cage, and where he is now** - before sunrise."

These three questions are the case's contract. The final board, the gathering and
the room contracts should all trace back to them.

## Finale: the gathering

After a valid final solve the game currently jumps to the end scene. Replace that with
a **gathering in the living room** (the cage room - the case circles back to where it
started; the cold open's location).

Device: the cat can't talk, so **Dikkie acts and Reyes interprets** (Columbo meets
Poirot). Dikkie knocks the dinner gong / sits in the empty cage until Reyes gives in and
calls everyone together. Each step: Dikkie *presents* something; Reyes says out loud what
it means; the suspect reacts.

### Structure (Poirot: suspicion on everyone, then the culprit)

| Step | Who | Player action | Payoff |
|------|-----|---------------|--------|
| 0 | Everyone | Gathering intro: the room, all ten faces, Reyes: "Fine. Cat. Show me." | Echoes the cold open |
| 1 | Chip | Present the evidence that clears him (commission: he loses money if the bird's gone) | Quick, comic warm-up |
| 2 | Derek | Present what breaks *his* certainty (the squawk was a recording) | The Big Lie is demolished in public |
| 3 | Rosa | Present the one-cart / two-rumbles evidence | The innocent is cleared - the stake from the opening pays off |
| 4 | Basil | Present the latch evidence -> he confesses to the latch (the false confession, Murder She Wrote midpoint) -> then present what he *saw* from behind the shed | The herring becomes the witness; the room turns |
| 5 | The Sound Guy | Present the timeline (6:30 / 6:47 / 7:00), then the means (cases + speaker), then the why | He cracks: the 47 takes confession |
| 6 | Rudebeak | The answer to "where is he now?" | A new emotional beat the player did not deduce (Takumi's surprise) |
| 7 | Button | Mirror of the opening (gate / sunrise / news van) | Final image |

Each herring step is the payoff of that herring's room confrontation, so the in-room
`requiresSolve` topics become the *evidence* used here - the gathering is the macro
level re-reading the meso level out loud.

### Implementation tiers

1. **Script only (cheapest):** rewrite the end-scene beats as the gathering with
   portraits and speakers. No interaction; drama comes from structure. Doable with the
   existing EndScene state.
2. **Staged final board (recommended):** split the final solve into 4-5 sequential
   accusation steps, each a small board (1-3 slots) with a reaction beat between them,
   the same deduction UI + portrait dialogue. This also solves the brute-force problem
   the research flagged: small steps can keep exact "N/M fit" feedback, and the seven-slot
   one-shot board goes away. The current final sentence gets cut into the steps above.
3. **Full staged scene:** the living room with all ten characters placed, Dikkie walks
   to each suspect to present evidence. Most dramatic, most work (new state, staging,
   possibly new poses).

Recommendation: build **tier 2** and render it over a living-room "gathering" background
with the cast lined up as static sprites (a cheap version of tier 3's look).

## Open questions for the designer

1. **Where is Rudebeak?** Marsh's slip says the cases are EMPTY, and the current end scene
   has the bird come home "salt-ruffled" the next morning without explaining it. The
   finale's "where is he now?" needs a concrete answer that is fair (planted) but not
   deducible on the final board - e.g. he escaped the case behind the shed and is roosting
   somewhere a clue already hints at (the pool area's bright feather?).
2. **Stake #3:** name the obvious suspect in the opening (stronger stakes, tilts a herring)
   or keep it vague?
3. **Roll-call device:** news van, Reyes's notepad, or something else?
4. **Opening art:** cards over the poster (no new art), or one exterior "front gate"
   background (new art, but the gathering and the opening could then both have a
   location)?
5. **Office prologue:** `Content/Levels/detective_office` (answering machine, newspaper,
   calendar - older "Lance"/"Pirate Cove 3" content) could become a short playable "the
   call" scene before the cold open. Keep it, cut it, or fold its premiere stake into the
   stakes card?
